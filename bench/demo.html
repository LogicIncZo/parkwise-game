03 · The receipt you can’t see

One 45-minute park, itemised

Fields below come straight from the spec; the linkage notes are inference — what a platform (or anyone it shares data with) can derive, not what the API document admits.

Collected by design

  • Precise location fixes — search latitude/longitude to the metre, plus radius and distance to every zone.
  • Vehicle registration plate — a state-assigned ID, logged per session with vehicle type.
  • Phone number — optional in the spec, requested by every real integration.
  • Email address — optional, same story.
  • Exact dwell time — start, end, extension and early-exit timestamps to the second.
  • Payment trail — amounts, currency, Juspay link tokens, payment status per event.

Derivable downstream inference

  • Plate → owner identity. VAHAN/RC records chain a plate to a name and address.
  • Phone → financial identity. A mobile number is the primary key of UPI, and usually of KYC.
  • Payment → instrument. The Juspay rail sees the payer’s instrument and tokenised credentials.
  • Routine profiling. Repeated sessions at the same zones and hours sketch a weekly movement pattern — no ANPR or GPS tracker required.
  • Sharing surface. “BAP integration” means third-party apps sit in this loop; the data’s blast radius is a policy choice, not a technical limit.

The asymmetry

  • The customer’s app shows a zone name and a countdown.
  • The platform’s ledger holds a person’s location, vehicle, contact and payment history — retained on the platform’s clock, under the city’s contract, with no user-visible expiry in the spec.
  • A parking fee is one of the smallest transactions in a city’s day. It is also one of the cheapest ways to timestamp a person’s presence at a place and time.
