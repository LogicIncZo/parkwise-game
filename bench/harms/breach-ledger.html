When the parking ledger leaks

Parking platforms have already lost tens of millions of users' plates, phones and addresses. The spec collects exactly that dataset — and binds nobody to any security duty.

The harm

Parking databases have a track record, and it is worse than their triviality suggests. In April 2021, ParkMobile — then the parking app of multiple US cities, including New York's DOT contracts — lost data on about 21 million users: email addresses, phone numbers, license plate numbers, mailing addresses and hashed passwords, circulated by the same criminal marketplace behind the Spirit Airlines and Plex breaches.[6][7] A class action followed.[8] In December 2023, EasyPark Group — which owns RingGo and ParkMobile across Europe — reported a breach stealing names, phone numbers, addresses, email addresses and parts of credit card numbers, reported to the UK ICO and European authorities.[9]

Notice what leaked both times: not card rails, but the presence layer — plates, phones, addresses. That is precisely the dataset this API collects per session. A breach of a parking ledger is not "just" spam fuel; it is a where-you-were-when dataset that supports stalking, vehicle theft targeting (plates of cars that reliably park overnight in a specific zone), and credential-stuffing into the phone-linked UPI ecosystem.

The spec's own security posture is thin by design — it is a public API document, not a security architecture. But its silence is the finding: nothing in the integration contract binds the platform to encryption-at-rest, breach-notification timelines, or independent audits. Under India's DPDP regime, those duties now sit in law, not in the spec — which is exactly why they must be verified in the contract.

Ask your city

  • Has the parking platform undergone an independent security audit (CERT-In empanelled), and will the summary be published?
  • What is the contractual breach-notification deadline to the ULB — and to affected parkers?
  • Does the platform need to store phone and email per session at all, or is a session token enough?
  • Who pays for notification and remediation when (not if) the vendor is breached — the city or the parkers?
