The harm

One session is a timestamp. A few weeks of sessions is a diary. The weekday 9:40 park next to the office zone; the Sunday morning zone near a place of worship; the late-night zone that keeps recurring; the sudden new zone across town. None of this requires exotic technology — just an ordinary database doing ordinary aggregation on fields the API collects by design.

To be precise about the epistemics: nothing in the spec says profiling happens, and nothing here claims a documented case of it. The claim is structural — the spec defines collection to the second, retains indefinitely by omission, and provides no endpoint for the parker to see, export, or erase what accumulated. The capability is the harm: it exists the moment anyone with database access decides to use it — a vendor's product team, a marketing "insights" product, or a future policy change nobody announced.

This is also where the asymmetry becomes concrete. The parker's app shows a countdown; the platform's ledger holds a movement pattern. India's DPDP Act grants data principals rights to correction and erasure[10] — but a right with no endpoint, no published process and no retention schedule is a right on paper. The API is where that paper either becomes real or visibly fails to.

Ask your city

  • What is the written retention period for session records — per field, including plate, phone and timestamps?
  • How does a parker exercise DPDP erasure on parking history — is there a form, an endpoint, an SLA?
  • Does the vendor run any analytics, "insights" or ML products on session history, for itself or for sale?
  • Are zone-level dashboards the only aggregate output, or do any reports include per-plate longitudinal views?
