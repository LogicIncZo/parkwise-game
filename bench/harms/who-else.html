The harm

Parking DPI is not one system; it is a relay. The ULB owns the curb and the concession; a vendor operates the platform; Beckn-style BAP apps resell discovery and booking; a payment institution carries the money; analytics and enforcement sit downstream. The public API is the visible edge of a contract stack that citizens never see — and each layer is a separate place where purpose can drift.

The governance layer finally has teeth: India's DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)), operationalising the 2023 Act.[10][12] The rollout is phased — consent-manager provisions by November 2026, and full operational compliance (notices, breach reporting, retention, security safeguards) by May 2027.[11] That timeline matters for parking precisely because these systems are being procured now: a concession signed today runs for years, so DPDP-grade obligations must be written into today's contracts, not retrofitted after May 2027.

The constructive demand is boring and radical at once: publish the contract stack. The concession agreement, the DPDP consent notice text shown in the app, the list of BAPs and sub-processors, and the retention schedule. Cities that publish these convert a surveillance surface into an accountable one; cities that don't have chosen opacity over a parking app — a trade no consumer ever got to make.

Ask your city

  • Which BAPs and third-party apps are live on the platform, and what data fields does each receive?
  • Is the concession agreement (with data-processing and audit clauses) published?
  • What DPDP notice is shown at consent — and does it name the vendor, the payment institution and retention periods?
  • Was the concession signed before or after DPDP Rules 2025 — and does it require DPDP-grade compliance ahead of the May 2027 deadline?
